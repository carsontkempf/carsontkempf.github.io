MODULE 10: ORBITAL MECHANICS
================================================================

1. DEFINITIONS
   1.1 Orbit
       - Path of an object under gravitational influence of a central body
       - Characterized by 6 orbital elements (shape, orientation, position)
       - Q: What shape is an orbit? (Ellipse -- circle is special case)

   1.2 Orbital Elements (Keplerian)
       - a: semi-major axis (size)
       - e: eccentricity (shape: 0=circle, 0<e<1=ellipse)
       - i: inclination (tilt relative to equator)
       - RAAN: right ascension of ascending node (rotation of orbit plane)
       - omega: argument of periapsis (orientation within orbit plane)
       - nu: true anomaly (position along orbit)
       - Q: LEO (ISS) a ~ 6780 km, GEO a ~ 42164 km. What altitude is GEO? (35,786 km)

   1.3 Orbital Velocity
       - Circular: v = sqrt(mu/r) where mu = G*M_earth = 3.986e14 m^3/s^2
       - LEO (~400km): v ~ 7.67 km/s (17,000 mph)
       - Q: Why does lower orbit = faster speed? (Stronger gravity, need more centripetal force)

   1.4 Period
       - T = 2*pi * sqrt(a^3/mu)
       - LEO: ~90 minutes
       - GEO: ~24 hours (why geostationary works)
       - Q: Double the altitude. How does period change? (Increases by factor 2*sqrt(2) ~ 2.83)

2. KEPLER'S LAWS
   2.1 First: orbits are ellipses with central body at one focus
   2.2 Second: line from body to satellite sweeps equal areas in equal times
       - Faster at perigee (closest), slower at apogee (farthest)
   2.3 Third: T^2 proportional to a^3
       - Universal: applies to all satellites of same central body

3. MANEUVERS
   3.1 Hohmann Transfer
       - Most fuel-efficient transfer between two circular orbits
       - Two burns: one to enter transfer ellipse, one to circularize
       - delta-v1 = v_transfer_perigee - v_circular_1
       - delta-v2 = v_circular_2 - v_transfer_apogee
       - Q: Transfer from 400km to 35786km LEO to GEO. Total delta-v? (~3.9 km/s)

   3.2 Plane Change
       - Changing inclination is EXPENSIVE (proportional to velocity * sin(angle_change))
       - Most efficient at apogee (where velocity is lowest)
       - Q: Why do we launch from near equator? (Free velocity from Earth rotation + avoids expensive plane changes)

   3.3 Station-Keeping
       - Small burns to maintain orbit against perturbations (drag, solar pressure, J2)
       - LEO: atmospheric drag lowers orbit (ISS reboosts every few weeks)
       - GEO: solar/lunar gravity perturbs inclination (need N-S stationkeeping)

4. WHY THIS MATTERS FOR WIRING
   - Orbit determines thermal environment (eclipse duration, solar intensity)
   - Orbit determines radiation environment (affects wire insulation degradation)
   - Orbit determines communication windows (drives antenna pointing requirements)
   - Orbit determines power budget (eclipse = battery only, sunlit = solar panels)
   - Q: In a 90-minute LEO orbit with 35 minutes of eclipse, what fraction of time is battery-powered? (39%)

5. GROUND TRACK & COMMUNICATION
   - Ground track: path traced on Earth's surface by sub-satellite point
   - Communication window: time satellite is above horizon from ground station
   - LEO satellite over a ground station: typically 5-10 minutes per pass
   - All data must be downlinked in those windows (drives data bus and antenna wiring)

6. HISTORICAL
   - Johannes Kepler (1571-1630): discovered three laws of planetary motion
       - Took 20 years of computation from Tycho Brahe's observations
       - "I have attested it as true in my deepest soul" -- on discovering elliptical orbits
   - Isaac Newton (1643-1727): explained WHY Kepler's laws work (gravity)
       - Principia Mathematica (1687): universal gravitation + calculus
   - Konstantin Tsiolkovsky (1857-1935): rocket equation, space station concepts
       - "Earth is the cradle of humanity, but one cannot stay in the cradle forever"
   - Sputnik (1957): first artificial satellite, 96-minute orbit, 65-degree inclination
   - Apollo 8 (1968): first humans in lunar orbit
       - Required precise orbital mechanics to reach Moon and return safely
   - GPS constellation: 24+ satellites in 6 orbital planes at 20,200 km
       - Orbital mechanics determines coverage geometry

MODULE 11: EVOLUTIONARY OPTIMIZATION FOR WIRE ROUTING
================================================================

1. DEFINITIONS
   1.1 Genetic Algorithm (GA)
       - Optimization method inspired by biological evolution
       - Population of candidate solutions
       - Selection: better solutions more likely to reproduce
       - Crossover: combine parts of two parent solutions
       - Mutation: random small changes to maintain diversity
       - Q: Why is this good for wire routing? (Many constraints, huge search space, no gradient available)

   1.2 Fitness Function
       - Quantifies how good a candidate solution is
       - For wire routing: f = w1*weight + w2*EMI_violations + w3*thermal_violations + w4*length
       - Lower is better (minimization)
       - Multi-objective: may have Pareto front of trade-offs

   1.3 Chromosome (Encoding)
       - How a solution is represented as data the GA can manipulate
       - Wire routing: sequence of waypoints for each harness segment
       - Or: assignment of wires to harness bundles (combinatorial)
       - Q: If 50 wires can be in any of 5 bundles, how many combinations? (5^50 ~ 10^34)

   1.4 Constraint Handling
       - Hard constraints: must be satisfied (wire gauge >= minimum for current)
       - Soft constraints: penalized in fitness (minimize voltage drop)
       - Penalty method: add large penalty to fitness for each violation
       - Repair method: fix violations after crossover/mutation

2. THE WIRE ROUTING PROBLEM
   2.1 Given:
       - Set of wires (source pin, destination pin, signal type, current)
       - Set of connectors (pin capacities, locations)
       - Physical layout (bays, trays, bulkheads, routing paths)
       - Constraints: EMI separation, thermal limits, bend radius, weight budget
   2.2 Find:
       - Assignment of wires to harness bundles
       - Routing path for each bundle through the vehicle
       - Minimizing: total weight + total length + EMI violations
   2.3 This is NP-hard (no polynomial-time exact solution exists)
       - Exhaustive search impossible for real systems (thousands of wires)
       - GAs find "good enough" solutions in reasonable time

3. GA IMPLEMENTATION
   3.1 Encoding: each chromosome = array of bundle assignments + route waypoints
   3.2 Initialization: random valid assignments (respecting hard constraints)
   3.3 Selection: tournament selection (pick k random, keep best)
   3.4 Crossover: uniform crossover for bundle assignment, order crossover for routes
   3.5 Mutation: swap wire between bundles, perturb waypoint position
   3.6 Termination: fixed generations, or convergence (no improvement for N generations)
   3.7 Typical parameters: population 100-500, generations 500-2000, crossover rate 0.8, mutation rate 0.05

4. MULTI-OBJECTIVE OPTIMIZATION
   4.1 Often no single best solution -- trade-offs between weight, EMI, thermal
   4.2 Pareto front: set of solutions where no objective can be improved without worsening another
   4.3 NSGA-II: popular multi-objective GA (Non-dominated Sorting GA)
   4.4 Engineer picks preferred solution from Pareto front based on priorities
   4.5 Q: If solution A weighs less but has worse EMI than solution B, which is better? (Neither -- both are Pareto-optimal. Engineer decides priority.)

5. WHY THIS IS "WHAT A SENIOR DEV COULDN'T DO"
   - Most wire routing in industry is manual (engineer draws paths by hand)
   - Tools like Capital Harness assist but don't optimize
   - Applying evolutionary computation to wire routing is RESEARCH-LEVEL
   - You're combining: graph theory + physics simulation + optimization theory + domain knowledge
   - The result: provably better harness designs (lighter, shorter, fewer violations)
   - This is the kind of feature Artifact's AI copilot would implement

6. HISTORICAL
   - Charles Darwin (1809-1882): theory of evolution by natural selection (1859)
   - John Holland (1929-2015): invented genetic algorithms (1960s-70s)
       - "Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems" (1975)
   - Hans-Paul Schwefel (1940-): evolution strategies (1960s, Germany)
   - David Goldberg (1953-): popularized GAs for engineering (1989 textbook)
   - Wire routing optimization: first attempts in PCB layout (1980s-90s)
       - Aerospace harness optimization is still largely manual -- opportunity is NOW

MODULE 12: CAPSTONE -- CUBESAT EWIS DESIGN
================================================================

1. THE PROJECT
   1.1 Design a complete Electrical Wiring Interconnect System for a 3U CubeSat
   1.2 Subsystems:
       - EPS (Electrical Power System): solar panels, batteries, regulators
       - OBC (On-Board Computer): processor, memory, data buses
       - ADCS (Attitude Determination & Control): magnetorquers, sun sensors, gyros
       - COMM (Communications): transceiver, antenna, amplifier
       - Payload (camera or science instrument)
       - Thermal (heaters, thermistors)

2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE (L0)
   2.1 Power bus topology
       - 3.3V regulated for digital logic
       - 5V for sensors
       - Unregulated battery bus (3.0-4.2V from Li-ion cells)
       - Solar panel input (variable, max ~8V for 3U panels)
   2.2 Data bus topology
       - I2C for slow sensors (100kHz-400kHz)
       - SPI for fast peripherals (up to 10MHz)
       - UART for ground communication interface
   2.3 Redundancy decisions
       - Dual-string EPS (two battery packs, cross-strapped)
       - Single-string payload (acceptable risk for science mission)
       - Critical: OBC watchdog timer resets system if software hangs

3. LOGICAL WIRING (L1)
   3.1 Pin-to-pin connection list (from-to table)
       - Example: EPS connector J1 pin 3 -> OBC connector J2 pin 7 (3.3V power)
       - Total: ~52 wire connections across 8 connectors
   3.2 Signal assignment rules
       - Power pins: largest gauge contacts (size 20, 7.5A rated)
       - Data pins: size 22 contacts (5A rated, only carrying mA)
       - Grounds: dedicated return for each subsystem
       - Spares: 2 per connector minimum

4. PHYSICAL HARNESS (L2)
   4.1 Bundle grouping
       - Group by routing path (wires that travel together)
       - Separate power harness from signal harness (EMI)
       - Typical: 3-5 harness segments in a 3U CubeSat
   4.2 Wire selection
       - Apply Module 2: voltage drop for each run
       - Apply Module 8: thermal derating for bundle size
       - Select AWG: usually 24-28 for signals, 20-22 for power
   4.3 Routing
       - CubeSat frame has specific routing channels along rails
       - Minimum bend radius: 10x wire diameter
       - Tie-down points every 3 inches (NASA-STD-8739.4)

5. ANALYSIS LAYER
   5.1 Voltage drop analysis (Module 2)
       - For EVERY power wire: compute V_drop = I * R_wire
       - Verify: drop < 3% of bus voltage at all loads
   5.2 Thermal analysis (Module 8)
       - For each harness segment: compute bundle center temperature
       - Verify: no wire exceeds its insulation rating
   5.3 EMI analysis (Module 4)
       - Verify: power and signal wires meet separation requirements
       - Verify: sensitive signals are shielded where required
   5.4 Redundancy analysis (Module 5)
       - Verify: no single connector failure kills the mission
       - Verify: redundant power paths are physically separated
   5.5 Weight budget
       - Sum all wire weight (mass per meter * length)
       - Compare to allocation (typically 5-10% of total spacecraft mass)
       - 3U CubeSat total ~4kg, wiring allocation ~200-400g

6. OPTIMIZATION (Module 11)
   6.1 Apply genetic algorithm to:
       - Wire-to-bundle assignment (minimize weight + EMI violations)
       - Route selection (minimize total harness length)
       - AWG selection (minimize weight while meeting voltage drop + thermal)
   6.2 Constraints:
       - All voltage drops < 3%
       - All temperatures < wire rating
       - All bundles meet EMI separation
       - Total weight < 400g budget
   6.3 Show: optimized design vs naive design (expected 15-25% weight savings)

7. DELIVERABLES (WHAT APPEARS ON THE WEBSITE)
   7.1 Interactive 3D CubeSat model with clickable subsystems
   7.2 Connection table (sortable, filterable)
   7.3 Harness routing visualization (color-coded by bundle)
   7.4 Analysis results dashboard (pass/fail per wire)

8. HISTORICAL
   - CubeSat standard: created 1999 by Bob Twiggs (Stanford) and Jordi Puig-Suari (Cal Poly)
       - Originally for university education, now used for real missions
       - Standard: 10cm x 10cm x 10cm = 1U. Mass < 1.33 kg per U.
   - First CubeSat launches: 2003 (6 satellites on one rocket)
   - Planet Labs: operates 200+ CubeSats for daily Earth imaging
       - Each one has a complete EWIS designed per these principles
   - NASA JPL MarCO (2018): first CubeSats to deep space (Mars)
       - Demonstrated CubeSat-class wiring can survive interplanetary environment
